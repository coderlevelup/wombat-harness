# Why Wombat?

> A poetry inspector for haikus and small language models. Don't get seduced into Thomas Hardy:
> we're keeping this strictly to the short stuff, not novels.

## The name

Wombat Harness is named after the name in the Monty Python sketch (S2E17,
[watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/gAobK3fyGDI?si=gSAyMI0Up1p2kinY&t=223)):

> "Oh, WOMBAT! Wombat Harness! Take me to that place where eternity knows no bounds!"

Some time after naming it, David realised the character in that sketch is a **poetry inspector**,
which made the name an even better fit for a harness for small language models. The README
already said it: "Python harness, not a novelist or a nude man."

## The metaphor

- The poetry inspector can't do novels.
- Novels stand for large language models: not what this inspector is supposed to be doing.
- His remit is the short stuff: haikus, and small language models.
- In David's words: "We're going to keep this strictly to small language models."

## Thomas Hardy: the wording matters

In the sketch, the woman tries to get the inspector into the bedroom to listen to Thomas Hardy.
The joke is being seduced *into* Thomas Hardy, not being seduced *by* Thomas Hardy himself.
Hence the line above: don't get seduced into Thomas Hardy. When a big model would make a task
easy, the point here is still to find out what a small one needs.

## What this is not

This is project lore and a statement of focus, delivered with humour. It is not a technical
restriction on which models the software can run: the engines and providers in the
[README](README.md#engines-and-providers) work with larger hosted models too, and
[ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) starts with a capable model before trying the same tasks on a tiny one.
